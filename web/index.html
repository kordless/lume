The Mathematics of Weber's Bracket

Translating Wilhelm Weber's classical force law to neural weight adjustments.

In Wilhelm Weber's classical electrodynamics (1846), the force between two moving charges depends on their relative distance, velocity ($v$), and acceleration ($a$):

$$W_{weber} = 1 - \frac{v^2}{2c^2} + \frac{v \cdot a}{c^2}$$

Where $c$ represents the characteristic speed of light (velocity scale). We adapt this physical bracket to dynamically scale weight updates:

  • Deceleration (Velocity squared): As velocity $v$ increases, the term $-\frac{v^2}{2c^2}$ acts as a kinetic friction stabilizer, slowing down parameters as they approach saddle points.
  • Momentum Boost (Velocity-acceleration dot product): The term $+\frac{v \cdot a}{c^2}$ injects an instant energy boost when velocity and acceleration align, accelerating the escape from flat plateaus.

import torch

def update_weber_bracket(lr, velocity, acceleration, c_sq=1.0):
    \"\"\"
    Applies Wilhelm Weber's electrodynamic bracket correction 
    to dynamically scale the base learning rate.
    
    W = 1 - v²/(2c²) + (v · a)/c²
    \"\"\"
    # Calculate relative velocity squared
    v_sq = torch.sum(velocity ** 2)
    
    # Calculate velocity-acceleration dot product
    v_dot_a = torch.sum(velocity * acceleration)
    
    # Compute the Weber bracket correction factor
    weber_bracket = 1.0 - (v_sq / (2.0 * c_sq)) + (v_dot_a / c_sq)
    
    # Clamp to prevent extreme learning rate reversal/instability
    weber_bracket = torch.clamp(weber_bracket, min=0.01, max=5.0)
    
    return lr * weber_bracket
